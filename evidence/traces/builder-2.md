### Item 3: material participation, active participation, passive losses

The question. 469(a) disallows a net passive activity loss. A rental activity is passive whatever the participation (469(c)(2), (c)(4)), except for a real estate professional (469(c)(7), which turns on hours). A trade or business activity is passive unless the taxpayer materially participates (469(c)(1)), that is, works in it on a regular, continuous and substantial basis (469(h)(1)). The $25,000 offset of 469(i) needs active participation in a rental. No household states hours in any activity, management decisions, or any participation status.

Decision. The rule's one test, applied to every activity alike: does a stated fact say that the person works in this activity? The board's work facts hold all year (prompts.py:18-21).
- Self-employment income (Schedule C, SSTB included) and farm operations income (Schedule F) say it. They are, by their names and by policyengine-us's documentation (self_employment_income "Self-employment non-farm income", 1402(a); farm_operations_income "Income from active farming operations"), income from the person's own work in their own business. A business that is the person's work throughout the year is regular and continuous involvement. No other individual's work in it is stated (an unlisted number is 0), so the person's participation is all of it, which is material participation under Temp. Reg. 1.469-5T(a)(2) (sources/cfr/lii-26-cfr-1.469-5T.txt). These activities are not passive.
- Partnership or S corporation income, rent and farm rent say that the person owns an interest or property, not that the person works in it. Participation is unstated, so false. Partnership and S corporation activities are passive (469(c)(1)); rentals are passive (469(c)(2)), with no real estate professional status (469(c)(7)) and no active participation, so no 469(i) offset (both comparers' position).
- Estate income as stated is the nonpassive part: policyengine-us documents the passive part as the separate input passive_estate_income, which no board or draw household states.
- The same status carries every provision that turns on it. 401(c)(2)(A)(i) (personal services a material income-producing factor) holds for Schedule C and F income, as the first maps already assume for the IRA, self-employed pension and 162(l) limits. 199A(i)'s minimum deduction (2026) counts only Schedule C and F qualified business income (active trades, material participation under 469(h)). Passive income is net investment income where it is not self-employment income (1411(c)(1)(A)(ii), (c)(2)(A), (c)(6)) and EITC disqualified income (32(i)(2)(D)): the partnership share that is not the partner's self-employment earnings.

Mechanics. Per return (spouses' activities together): net all passive amounts (partnership and S corporation, rent, farm rent). A net gain stands. A net loss is suspended: the losses allowed equal the passive income, each loss activity keeping a ratable share (1.469-1T(f)(2)(i)); the rest carries forward to the next year as a deduction of the same activity (469(b)), and no household states a loss carried in from an earlier year, so none comes in. A disallowed loss counts for no subtitle A tax, so it leaves net earnings from self-employment too (1.469-1T(d)(3)): a partner's box 14 loss shrinks with the activity's allowed share. The allowed amounts replace the stated ones in every input of both maps.

The alternative (strict): material participation is unlisted in every case, so false for every activity: Schedule C and F become passive too. That suspends 033's SSTB loss of 11,400, 039's Schedule C loss of 6,260 and 042's farm loss of 1,127 (none has passive income), counts none of their income toward 199A(i), and, applied as uniformly, ends 401(c)(2) earned income for every Schedule C filer (no 162(l), no self-employed pension deduction, no IRA compensation from the business). I do not take it: self-employment states the person's own work, and reading it as stating nothing would leave the stated name without meaning. A second alternative (generous): the only-participant logic of 1.469-5T(a)(2) also makes the owner of a partnership interest or a rental the only participant, so nothing is passive except rentals beyond the 469(i) offset; that infers work from ownership, which the rule forbids. Hours worked last week, stated for some filers (004's head works 40 hours with no wages and only a partnership loss of 135), are not tied to any activity, so they do not state participation in the partnership.

### Item 8: rent, farm rent and qualified business income

What the households state. Rent ("rental income", policyengine-us: "Income from rental of property"), farm rent ("farm rental income", no documentation; reference 26 U.S.C. 1402) and, for some persons, the unadjusted basis of qualified property ("unadjusted basis of qualified property" in the prompt, prompts.py INPUT_LABEL_OVERRIDES; policyengine-us: "Share of unadjusted basis upon acquisition of all property held by qualified pass-through businesses", reference 199A(b)(6)). On the board, rent or farm rent with that basis and no other business: 022 (rent 4,550, basis 24,786), 076 (rent 4,000, basis 49,921), 062 (farm rent 4,792.94, basis 22,440), 112 (farm rent 1,920, basis 54,381). Rent or farm rent without it: 083, 098, 120.

The law. Qualified business income is the net income of a qualified trade or business (199A(c)(1)); a qualified trade or business is any trade or business other than a specified service trade or business or an employee's work (199A(d)(1)); the regulations define trade or business as a section 162 trade or business (Treas. Reg. 1.199A-1(b)(14)); a rental counts when it rises to a 162 trade or business (or meets the safe harbor of Rev. Proc. 2019-38, sources/irs/rp-19-38.txt, which needs 250 hours of rental services a year and separate books). Qualified property is tangible depreciable property "held by, and available for use in, the qualified trade or business" (199A(b)(6)(A)). The first maps treated rent as not qualified (READINGS rental_qbi) but farm rent as qualified always, which the comparers call inconsistent; both are rentals, and nothing in the law treats them differently.

Decision (one rule for rent and farm rent). A rental is a qualified trade or business when the person states the unadjusted basis of qualified property (not the SSTB basis) and states no other activity that is a trade or business (self-employment, farm operations, partnership or S corporation income). Reason: by definition, qualified property is held by a qualified trade or business, so a stated basis states that the person has one; the person's activities are the stated ones (an unlisted number is 0, an unlisted fact false); when the only stated activities that could hold the property are rentals, the rentals are that business. When another trade or business is stated, the basis is explained by it, and nothing states that the rental is a trade or business, so it is not. Without a stated basis, a rental is not a trade or business: the 162 facts and the safe harbor's 250 hours are unstated. When a person states both rent and farm rent with a basis and no other business, both are read as qualified (no stated fact separates them; one draw household). A basis with no rental and no business (one draw household) qualifies nothing. Estate income is not qualified business income (whether the estate carries a trade or business is unstated; policyengine-us's default agrees). Partnership and S corporation income is qualified business income, as both first maps and policyengine-us's default read it: the Schedule E line for partnership and S corporation income carries the entities' business income (K-1 box 1, ordinary business income); this reading is noted.

Effect. 022 and 076 (rent) become qualified business income; 062 and 112 (farm rent) stay qualified; 083 and 120 (no basis) stay not qualified; 120's farm rent, without a basis, stops being qualified (it moves no deduction: its net passive amount is a suspended loss). 076 is the scored cell this turns.

The alternative (strict): whether a rental is a 162 trade or business is unstated, so false for every rental, basis or not: 022 and 076 stay not qualified, and 062 and 112 farm rent stop being qualified. It reads the stated basis as a fact without effect. The other alternative (policyengine-us's default): every rental is qualified (rental_income_would_be_qualified and farm_rent_income_would_be_qualified default to true), which needs the 162 facts the rule makes false for 083 and 120.

Routes. Tax-Calculator: a qualified rental goes to e27200 (which Tax-Calculator counts in e02000, in qualified business income and in net investment income); a rental that is not qualified goes to e02000 only. TAXSIM: a qualified rental goes to `scorp` (qualified business income, no self-employment tax; ordinary, net investment and EITC investment income); one that is not goes to `intrec`.
