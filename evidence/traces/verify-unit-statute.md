### F3 (P2) Roth 401(k) deferrals are not limited to compensation, so a filer with no wages counts as an active participant and as a saver's-credit contributor
- unit.py:136-138 limits only the traditional deferral to wages; the Roth deferral keeps its 402(g)-scaled value whatever the wages. Filer.active_participant (unit.py:101-103) then turns on from the Roth amount alone. Elective deferrals are made out of compensation (402(g)(3) elective deferrals; the annual-additions limit of 415(c)(1)(B) is 100% of compensation: inference from the statute's structure, 415 not saved), so a person with no wages and no self-employment cannot make one.
- Board households hit (scratch scan.py): scenario_003 head (64, wages 0, Roth 4,222.20 kept), 004 head (62, Roth 2,724.00), 073 head (57, Roth 980.64), 085 head (67, Roth 1.50), 089 head (47, Roth 953.40); all have zero wages and zero SE income. It would move a household with no wages, Roth 401(k) desired, AGI under the saver's ceilings and tax to absorb the credit, or a joint return where the no-wage spouse's false active-participant status phases out a spousal IRA deduction above 129,000 instead of 242,000.
- PolicyEngine differs in the other direction: it limits neither deferral to wages (pe-income.md:76 J1), so its saver's credit and active-participant test count the full desired traditional amount too.
- Fix: in `_filer`, cap traditional + Roth together at wages (scale both in proportion, or Roth after traditional), note it, and add a test: wages 0 implies deferral = roth_deferral = 0 and active_participant false.
